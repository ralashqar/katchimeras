# Katchimera Art Bible

This document is the working art bible for Katchimeras.

Use it as the source of truth for:

- prompt writing
- art review
- future illustration and 3D outsourcing
- onboarding showcase selection
- deciding whether a new creature feels like it belongs in the same world

## 1. Product Fit

Katchimeras are not battle units, toy mascots, or generic mobile game creatures.

They are:

- premium stylized mascots
- memory-shaped encounter companions
- soft magical collectibles that reflect real life

The app turns real places, routines, and moments into creatures the user wants to keep, revisit, and share. The art therefore needs to feel:

- cute
- relatable
- collectible
- premium
- emotionally readable
- calm enough for reflection
- alive in pose and acting

The correct emotional target is:

- `I want to keep this`

Not:

- `I want to fight with this`
- `This is a random funny mascot`
- `This is a kids-game toy`

## 2. Core Style Direction

Recommended default style:

- `premium stylized 3D mascot creature render`

Expanded phrasing:

- `premium stylized game mascot CGI`
- `Fortnite-inspired silhouette confidence without hero aggression`
- `Royal Match-inspired facial charm and readability`
- `clean premium mobile-game mascot render`

This is the right middle ground between:

- too realistic
- too toy-like
- too chibi
- too game-hero

The operating rule is:

- `stylized shape, believable light`

Push exaggeration in anatomy, silhouette, pose, and expression.
Keep realism in lighting response, shadow logic, eye wetness, and render polish.

## 3. Non-Negotiable Shared Signature

Every Katchimera should share the same visual DNA, even when the encounter theme changes radically.

The lineup signature is:

1. `signature eyes`
2. `visible glow core`
3. `head-dominant rounded mascot silhouette`
4. `tactile premium materials`
5. `one overscaled encounter motif`

If a creature misses those five things, it will likely drift off-model.

## 4. Signature Eyes

This is the strongest shared feature across the line.

### Eye design rules

- eyes should be much larger than in the current set
- eyes should dominate the face read without turning into extreme chibi dots
- eye shape should be rounded almond or rounded teardrop, not anime-wide
- pupils should feel glossy and luminous
- eyes should carry a warm internal light
- eyes should read clearly at thumbnail size
- eyelids should be soft and friendly
- brows should be tiny, gentle, and expressive

### Emotional target

The face should read as:

- kind
- curious
- safe
- slightly wistful
- adoptable

Not:

- manic
- smug
- chaotic
- hyperactive
- vacant toy face
- predator face

The expression should be stronger and more graphic than a figurine render.
Think readable mascot acting, not neutral premium posing.

### Best implementation rule

Default to:

- dramatically oversized glossy eyes
- tiny mouth
- soft cheek treatment
- subtle brow lift
- stronger eye-light and catchlight than a neutral figurine render

Useful expression presets:

- delighted
- smug-cute
- shy
- sleepy
- startled
- proud
- overfocused

## 5. Glow Core

Every Katchimera should visibly contain some form of life-energy.

This is the second major unifier after the eyes.

### Glow-core rule

Each creature should have one readable internal light source, such as:

- chest glow
- belly glow
- throat lantern
- horn light
- cup-center glow
- ember core
- pearl core
- firefly core

### Why it matters

The creature is not just themed after a place.
It is a day-memory brought to life.
The glow core makes the creature feel alive, magical, and emotionally warm.

### Important constraint

The glow should feel contained and premium.

Avoid:

- full-body neon flooding
- game-VFX overload
- electricity everywhere
- hard sci-fi energy effects unless the encounter specifically earns it

## 6. Silhouette And Body Proportions

Katchimeras should feel collectible, huggable, and instantly readable, not combat-ready.

### Shared body language

- clearly top-heavy
- rounded torso
- dynamic, appealing stance or gentle hover
- short chunky limbs
- soft transitions between forms
- clear silhouette at small size

### Proportion target

Use:

- head and face importance around 45 to 55 percent of total read
- eye read around 30 to 38 percent of the face
- body mass simple enough to read instantly
- limbs shortened clearly from realism
- hands, paws, ears, horns, tails simplified into bold readable shapes
- paws and feet larger and cuter than realistic anatomy
- one hero feature should be intentionally oversized beyond realism
- posing should imply motion, attitude, or charm rather than static display

Avoid:

- long heroic legs
- sharp predatory anatomy
- tiny head, big body realism
- super-deformed bobblehead proportions
- static museum-figurine posing

### Pose families

Use one clear pose family per render:

- bounce pose
- mid-hop pose
- proud pose
- clingy pose
- sneak pose
- slouchy cozy pose

Every pose should include:

- asymmetry
- head tilt or torso turn
- implied motion or attitude
- readable silhouette at thumbnail size

## 7. Material Language

The premium feeling should come from tactile material treatment, not complexity for its own sake.

### Default material family

Use one or two dominant materials per creature:

- plush velvet
- brushed ceramic
- matte resin
- soft moss
- bark with softened edges
- polished shell
- warm pastry texture
- felted fiber
- brushed metal accents

### Material rules

- surfaces should feel touchable
- highlights should feel clean, simplified, and premium
- avoid cheap plastic shine
- avoid photoreal material complexity
- avoid moody cinematic realism
- avoid too many competing materials

### Render recipe

- use clean key, fill, rim, and bounce light
- keep grounded contact shadows under chin, paws, and feet
- keep eye gloss and glow-core shading richer than the rest of the body
- let the hero motif carry the second-richest shading treatment
- keep body surfaces broader and cleaner

### Read as

- keepsake
- premium figurine
- crafted charm
- magical companion object

## 8. Motif Discipline

Each creature should express one dominant encounter idea fast.

Examples:

- park -> leaf ears
- coffee shop -> ceramic cup hood
- bakery -> brioche ears or pastry body
- farm -> straw mane
- riverwalk -> glassy ripple tail
- landmark rare -> filigree crown or skyline mantle

### Rule

One oversized main motif, one secondary support detail, one glow-core placement.

That is enough.

Avoid:

- stacking five props
- costume-like clutter
- literal scene recreation on the body
- realistic environmental dressing that makes the creature feel like a render test instead of a mascot

## 9. Category Handling

Katchimeras do not need to be mostly animals.
They can be:

- foodlings
- objectlings
- spirits
- hybrid mascots
- place-beasts

But they should all still obey the same face, glow-core, silhouette, and materials system.

### Important category rule

The line should feel like one brand family, not several unrelated franchises.

That means:

- food creatures still use signature eyes and glow core
- object creatures still feel soft and alive
- landmark rares still stay creature-first, not doll-first

## 10. Human-Likeness Boundary

The app works best when creatures feel personal without becoming literal characters.

### Use

- creature silhouettes
- mascot hybrids
- spirit-companion forms

### Avoid

- princess-doll reads
- humanoid fashion-character reads
- fully human faces
- stylized little people in costumes

Landmark rares can become more regal, but they should still remain clearly within the Katchimera family and avoid turning into human dolls.

## 11. Stage Direction

The same family should evolve without losing its core face language.

### Sprout

- simplest silhouette
- cutest face
- biggest eye-to-body ratio
- cleanest motif expression
- lowest detail density

### Shaped

- slightly richer markings
- more confident proportions
- stronger motif integration
- still highly readable

### Ascended

- more elegant
- more aura
- more refined surface detailing
- more dramatic silhouette extension

Do not let ascended forms become:

- harsh
- spiky
- villainous
- overloaded

## 12. Rarity Direction

Rarity should feel premium, not noisy.

### Common

- simple motif
- soft glow
- clean graphic silhouette

### Rare

- stronger material richness
- more distinct glow treatment
- one elegant secondary detail

### Epic

- elevated aura
- stronger habitat integration
- more striking silhouette rhythm

### Legendary

- unmistakable centerpiece
- richer lighting and filigree
- stronger emotional gravity

Legendary should not mean:

- explosive effects
- visual chaos
- unreadable complexity

## 13. Background And Camera

Katchimeras live inside a premium reveal system.
They should be composed like collectible portraits.

### Composition defaults

- one creature only
- centered or near-centered
- three-quarter view by default
- slight pedestal, float, or planted grounding
- dark atmospheric background
- only a hint of habitat
- pose should feel clear and mascot-like rather than realistic

### Background rule

The background should support the encounter theme without becoming scenery-first.

Examples:

- cafe -> soft amber bokeh
- park -> dark green hush
- urban -> restrained reflected neon
- waterside -> horizon mist glow

Avoid:

- busy scenes
- literal environment dumps
- white ecommerce background
- overbuilt diorama sets

## 14. Shareability Rules

These creatures will live in onboarding, the home hero, timeline thumbnails, and share cards.

So every approved render should pass these checks:

- recognizable at thumbnail size
- readable in one second
- emotionally charming on first glance
- strong enough to post or share without explanation

If the creature only works when viewed large, it is not strong enough.

## 15. Do / Don't

### Do

- make creatures feel like premium memory mascots
- use large luminous eyes
- show a contained inner glow
- simplify the silhouette before adding detail
- keep one dominant motif
- make materials tactile and expensive-feeling
- preserve calm magical atmosphere
- push proportions far enough to feel intentional

### Don't

- make them feel like fighters
- over-index on action-pose energy
- use flat sticker-neon as the main identity
- push into toy-commercial plastic
- rely on costume clutter
- humanize landmark creatures too far
- make everything equally loud
- leave them in an awkward middle between realistic and mascot-like

## 16. Current Repo Read

Strong direction in the current set:

- `Mossprout`
- `Lattelet`
- `Crumbun`
- `Ironette`

Why they work:

- clear motif
- tactile premium finish
- friendly face
- strong silhouette
- highly shareable

Riskier or off-model tendencies to avoid:

- battle-hero energy
- over-neon signboard treatment
- princess-doll landmark treatment

## 17. Prompt Architecture

Recommended prompt structure:

1. creature identity
2. encounter theme
3. one dominant motif
4. face and eye treatment
5. glow-core placement
6. material language
7. silhouette and pose
8. lighting and background
9. negative constraints

### Core reusable style block

Use language close to:

- `premium stylized mascot creature CGI`
- `slightly oversized glossy luminous eyes`
- `visible inner glow core`
- `rounded mascot silhouette`
- `soft cinematic lighting`
- `clean readable proportions`
- `tactile premium materials`
- `dark atmospheric background`
- `emotionally readable face`

### Example base prompt

`A premium stylized mascot creature shaped by a cozy cafe encounter, with a ceramic cup hood and warm caramel glow core, slightly oversized glossy luminous eyes, tiny mouth, rounded mascot silhouette, short chunky limbs, tactile ceramic and plush textures, soft cinematic lighting, dark atmospheric background, calm magical mood, single centered portrait, no text, no watermark, no extra characters.`

## 18. Prompt Templates By Encounter Type

### Nature / park

`A premium stylized mascot creature shaped by a park encounter, with [motif], slightly oversized glossy luminous eyes, visible [glow-core placement], rounded mascot silhouette, short chunky limbs, tactile moss and soft bark materials, calm magical mood, soft cinematic lighting, dark atmospheric background with subtle green habitat hint, single centered portrait, no text, no extra characters.`

### Cozy / cafe / bakery

`A premium stylized mascot creature shaped by a cozy food or drink encounter, with [motif], slightly oversized glossy luminous eyes, visible [glow-core placement], rounded mascot silhouette, tactile ceramic, pastry, plush, or warm resin materials, soft amber lighting, dark atmospheric background, premium stylized render, no text, no extra characters.`

### Urban

`A premium stylized mascot creature shaped by an urban encounter, with [motif], slightly oversized glossy luminous eyes, contained inner glow core, rounded mascot silhouette, tactile polished resin and brushed metal accents, restrained reflected neon, calm metropolitan magical mood, dark atmospheric background, no text, no extra characters.`

### Landmark rare

`A premium stylized mascot creature shaped by a rare landmark encounter, with [motif], slightly oversized glossy luminous eyes, visible inner glow core, regal but still creature-first silhouette, tactile premium filigree and softened luxury materials, elegant cinematic lighting, dark atmospheric background with subtle landmark memory hint, no text, no extra characters, no human doll styling.`

## 19. Review Checklist

Before approving a new Katchimera, ask:

1. Does it read as a Katchimera in one second?
2. Are the eyes clearly part of the same family?
3. Is the glow core visible and controlled?
4. Does the silhouette feel rounded and collectible?
5. Is there one dominant motif instead of clutter?
6. Does it feel premium rather than gamey?
7. Is it shareable without explanation?

If the answer is not clearly yes to most of these, revise the prompt.

## 20. Final Direction

The best signature for Katchimeras is not just `big eyes`.

It is:

- `signature luminous eyes`
- `visible inner glow core`
- `rounded mascot silhouette`
- `tactile premium materials`
- `one clear encounter motif`

That combination makes the line:

- cute
- relatable
- premium
- collectible
- emotionally coherent
- unmistakably Katchimera
