# Companion Editorial Rollout

This tracker applies the standards in
[`companion-content-authoring-guide.md`](./companion-content-authoring-guide.md)
to every Journey companion. A batch is complete only after its Focus flow,
rotating content, bond dialogue, quick goals, quests, variants, migrations, and
tests have passed editorial and repository review.

## Batch workflow

Each batch has two reviewable stages.

### A. Audit and content design

1. Inventory every visible string and persisted answer.
2. Define the family boundary, benefit, voice, and pressure risks.
3. Identify question/answer mismatches and duplicated concepts.
4. Check quest wording against its evidence contract.
5. Produce a complete authored pack, not isolated corrections.

### B. Integration and verification

1. Integrate the authored content under stable IDs where meaning is compatible.
2. Increment changed Journey versions.
3. Migrate old system-generated Focus titles.
4. Refresh changed unfinished check-ins while preserving completed history.
5. Add family-specific editorial and evidence tests.
6. Run the full repository check.
7. Read the experience on a small device and with a screen reader.

## Shared findings outside Steppling

- Four pilot families have only four hand-written daily prompts. Their later
  prompts are generated from abstract role tags and repeat after four concepts.
- Generated daily prompts use one generic answer set even when the answers do
  not address the question.
- Reviews share one answer set across four different review questions.
- Return conversations share one answer set even when asking about goals,
  reasons, barriers, and completion.
- Bond moments are structurally sound after the Steppling repair, but their
  prose and answers remain generic rather than family-specific.
- Default quest variants append “choose one detail you have not used before” or
  “notice what changed” to activities where that instruction may not match the
  evidence type.
- Some quick goals are clear actions; others are vague intentions, assume
  access, or add pressure through frequency and performance wording.

## Batch 1 audit

### Batch 1 implementation note

Completed in August 2026. Each family now has 12 authored daily pulses, four
reviews, four return conversations, and three family-specific bond moments.
Their Focus journeys are version 3, with unfinished conversations refreshed by
the shared Journey normaliser while completed answers and goals remain in
history. Quick goals were expanded or revised to stay concrete and optional.
Repeatable real-life quests now use evidence-matched authored variants instead
of the generic “new detail” fallback.

### Sleep/Rest — Bedrotte and Snoozle

**Life area:** sleep rhythm, winding down, restorative downtime, recovery, and
permission to stop.

**Voice:** soft, protective, unhurried, and practical. It must not sound like a
sleep clinician or promise that a ritual will improve sleep.

**Keep:** the current branching Focus recognises that rest is broader than
sleep. Its paths for sleep rhythm, wind-down, recovery, downtime, and boundaries
are more useful than a single generic three-question flow.

**Rewrite needs:**

- shorten repeated and abstract helper text;
- distinguish “I needed rest” from “rest restored me”;
- allow difficult nights, interrupted rest, caring duties, and no clear change;
- avoid making bedtime consistency the universally best answer;
- write 12 distinct prompts covering signals, conditions, boundaries, comfort,
  recovery, and what did or did not help;
- make every review and return answer specific to its question;
- author Bedrotte/Snoozle bond moments around the kind of support the user wants;
- audit quests for medical implication, screen blame, and unverifiable claims;
- keep Vesperitt's boundary clear: Rest owns stopping and recovery, while
  Vesperitt owns intentional life after dark.

### Tasklet

**Life area:** meaningful work, focus, projects, and next actions.

**Voice:** capable, clarifying, lightly playful, and never managerial.

**Pressure risks:** burnout, perfectionism, executive dysfunction, unstable
energy, work becoming personal worth, and “productive” being treated as morally
better.

**Rewrite needs:**

- move the Focus from project identity to one useful outcome and one realistic
  next condition;
- include “I needed to stop”, “the task changed”, and “nothing moved today” as
  valid information;
- distinguish choosing, clarifying, starting, continuing, finishing, delegating,
  and dropping work;
- avoid calling every unfinished task friction or resistance;
- ensure quick goals are observable and small without becoming busywork;
- make quests verify a completed action without demanding artificial output;
- write bond progression around preferred support, learned working conditions,
  and sustainable ways of moving work forward.

### Mossprout

**Life area:** nearby nature, living details, seasons, and accessible green or
outdoor moments.

**Voice:** grounded, curious, sensory, and delighted by small changes.

**Access risks:** no nearby green space, unsafe weather, limited mobility,
allergies, caring duties, darkness, or only being able to notice nature through
a window or cared-for plant.

**Rewrite needs:**

- retain the strong sensory pilot voice but replace its generic answers;
- make “indoors looking out”, a houseplant, street tree, or no nature moment
  valid where the evidence contract permits;
- avoid implying that going outdoors is always healthier, calmer, or possible;
- distinguish noticing, returning, caring, seasonal change, and sensory effect;
- make photo variants ask for visually collectable differences while note
  variants ask for observations;
- avoid duplicating “notice one living thing” across quick goals and quests;
- make bond progression about how the user notices and relates to nearby nature,
  not about visiting increasingly impressive places.

### Gatherglow

**Life area:** reciprocal friendship, social connection, attention, and
belonging. It does not own caregiving or merely being around people.

**Voice:** warm, socially perceptive, invitational, and respectful of boundaries.

**Wellness risks:** loneliness, grief, estrangement, rejection, social anxiety,
limited social energy, unsafe relationships, and implying that the user must
repair every connection.

**Rewrite needs:**

- ask what kind of connection the user wants without assuming more contact is
  always better;
- include solitude, boundaries, unanswered outreach, and limited energy as valid;
- distinguish reaching out, receiving contact, shared attention, reciprocity,
  repair, appreciation, and belonging;
- never instruct the user to disclose more or contact someone unsafe;
- make quick goals optional, specific, and under the user's control;
- avoid capturing or storing another person's private information in quest copy;
- write bond progression around preferred social support, learned patterns of
  connection, and relationships or values worth tending.

## Catalogue-wide consistency audit

Completed in August 2026. All 54 playable families now have a versioned Focus
Journey, a coherent three-question direction flow, actionable result titles,
at least two relevant task suggestions per result, at least eight quick goals,
and a complete 23-item rotating content pack. The 29 former specialist gaps now
use the same invitation, questionnaire, goal, check-in, review, return, and bond
systems as the original Journey families.

Those 29 families retain their 116 family-owned quests across bond levels 1,

remain a separate enhancement for families whose role metadata still names a
planned activity; their absence no longer removes or weakens Focus, goals,
prompts, bond dialogue, or real-life progression.

### Shellio: swimming, beach, and water connection

Shellio owns the broad water relationship. Its Focus supports five directions:
a sustainable swimming rhythm, safer water confidence, swimming as enjoyable
movement, beach or shoreline time, and connecting with water without entering.
The system treats pool swimming, supervised or lifeguarded open-water swimming,
shore visits, and non-entry views as different valid experiences.

Shellio must never imply that entering water is required for progress. Questions
and invitations should account for swimming ability, fear or confidence, health
and mobility, recovery, lesson or supervision needs, access and cost, weather,
water quality, local advice, equipment, travel, and caring duties. Open-water
copy must not present a generic swimming goal as permission to swim alone or
beyond the player's ability. Choosing a pool, qualified lesson, suitable depth,
shore-only visit, different day, or no water task is valid progression data.

All repeatable real-life quests receive three stable presentation lenses. The
former generic instructions to find an unused detail or compare with last time
have been removed. Unauthored lenses instead offer an ordinary or adapted
return and a safe, accessible alternative without changing what the quest
evidence can prove.

## Definition of complete

For every family in a batch:

- all 23 rotating prompts are unique and authored;
- each question has bespoke, coherent answers;
- the Focus follows a meaningful decision sequence;
- optionality and a low-pressure path are present;
- family boundaries remain distinct;
- quests and variants match their evidence contracts;
- quick goals are concrete, safe, and realistically repeatable;
- changed persisted content has versioning or migration coverage;
- family-specific editorial tests pass;
- the full repository check passes.
