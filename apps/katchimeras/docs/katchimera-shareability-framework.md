# Katchimera Shareability Framework

This document defines what makes a Katchimera more likely to be:

- posted
- sent to friends
- screenshotted
- remembered
- understood instantly by someone outside the app

It also defines how Katchimeras should be assembled from life signals so they feel like flattering self-portraits rather than random generated creatures.

## 1. Core Rule

Do not generate a creature from data.

Generate:

- a lovable self-portrait from a day pattern

The creature should make the user think:

- `this is me`
- `this is weirdly accurate`
- `this is cute enough to post`
- `someone else will get this immediately`

## 2. What Makes A Katchimera Shareable

The most shareable Katchimeras combine four things:

1. `instant visual read`
2. `identity recognition`
3. `flattering truth`
4. `specific life detail`

### Instant visual read

The image should be legible in under a second.

That usually means:

- big eyes
- strong silhouette
- one dominant motif
- clear palette
- no clutter

### Identity recognition

The user should understand why they got it.

Better:

- `my coffee creature`
- `my rainy park creature`
- `my night-city creature`

Worse:

- `my calm-energy-exploration hybrid`

### Flattering truth

The app should feel accurate, but slightly elevating.

It should say:

- `we saw something real`
- `we made it beautiful`

It should not feel like:

- raw tracking data
- fitness stats disguised as fantasy
- rigid personality labeling

### Specific life detail

The most postable creatures come from recognizable life patterns:

- cafe regular
- bakery stop
- park walker
- late-night route
- museum visit
- recovery day
- big running day

These are easier to share than abstract trait blends.

## 3. The Shareability Stack

Each Katchimera should be assembled using this hierarchy:

1. `Encounter identity`
2. `Emotional tone`
3. `Pattern meaning`
4. `Visible motif package`
5. `Rarity / twist`

### 3.1 Encounter identity

This should drive most of the creature.

Recommended weighting:

- `60 to 70 percent`

Examples:

- independent cafe
- bakery
- park
- bookstore
- riverwalk
- run session
- late-night city route

This gives the creature immediate social legibility.

### 3.2 Emotional tone

This determines how the creature feels, not what it is.

Recommended weighting:

- `15 to 20 percent`

Examples:

- cozy
- grounded
- curious
- bright
- reflective
- restorative
- charged

This should influence:

- face softness
- posture
- glow behavior
- material warmth
- color bias

### 3.3 Pattern meaning

This is the hidden reason the creature matters.

Recommended weighting:

- `10 to 15 percent`

Examples:

- return
- recovery
- momentum
- discipline
- comfort
- exploration
- social warmth

This is the part that turns a cute creature into a life reflection.

### 3.4 Visible motif package

Turn the day pattern into two or three visible traits.

Examples:

- cafe + cozy + return
- `cup hood`, `caramel glow`, `soft scarf`

- park + recovery + repetition
- `moss ears`, `grounded paws`, `dew core`

- night route + urban + identity
- `signal tail`, `streetlight blush`, `midnight glow core`

The user should be able to explain the creature in one sentence.

### 3.5 Rarity / twist

Rarity should come from meaningful distinction, not randomness.

Good rarity sources:

- unusual place
- unusually intense day
- meaningful break from weekly pattern
- rare encounter location
- strong emotional contrast

Bad rarity sources:

- arbitrary drop tables
- hidden score thresholds with no visible reason

## 4. Recommended Generation Formula

Use this model:

`Encounter -> Mood -> Pattern -> Motif -> Reveal Copy`

### Example

Input:

- encounter: `independent cafe`
- mood: `cozy`
- pattern: `return`

Output:

- creature type: `foodling / mascot hybrid`
- motifs: `cup hood`, `latte swirl`, `soft caramel glow`
- reflection line: `Warmth, routine, and a little wandering shaped this one.`

This is stronger than:

- `energy 0.62 calm 0.54 exploration 0.31`

## 5. Trait Assembly Rules

### Rule 1

One Katchimera should have:

- one primary encounter identity
- one emotional tone
- one deeper life-pattern meaning

Not five equal ideas competing.

### Rule 2

At least one visible trait must explain the encounter.

Examples:

- bakery -> crust ears
- riverwalk -> ripple tail
- park -> moss hood
- basketball -> court-line markings

### Rule 3

At least one visible trait must explain the emotional tone.

Examples:

- restorative -> lowered shoulders, softer cheeks, dimmer warm glow
- energetic -> brighter core, lifted posture, sharper forward motion
- grounded -> broader stance, calmer weight, lower center of gravity

### Rule 4

At least one copy line must explain the life-pattern meaning.

Examples:

- return -> `Your familiar places are starting to leave a signature.`
- recovery -> `Stillness became the part of the day that stayed visible.`
- momentum -> `The day kept pulling forward and the hatch answered.`

## 6. Viral-Friendly Archetypes

The best archetypes are the ones people already self-identify with.

### Routine archetypes

- coffee regular
- bakery regular
- park walker
- late-night stroller
- bookstore person
- riverside person
- gym / running person

### Mood archetypes

- cozy day
- soft day
- bright day
- charged day
- restorative day
- grounded day

### Socially legible archetypes

- sushi person
- museum person
- dog-park person
- basketball person
- bubble-tea person
- traveler

### Rare main-character archetypes

- landmark rare
- unusually huge effort day
- unusually serene day
- unexpected combination day
- meaningful break from routine

These create the strongest `I need to post this` moments.

## 8. Reveal Copy Framework

The reveal line is a major part of shareability.

It should be:

- short
- flattering
- specific
- legible outside the app

### Bad

- `Your calm and exploration scores were balanced.`

### Better

- `A warm stop and a little wandering shaped this one.`

### Best

- `Your cafe rituals are starting to leave a signature.`

## 9. Copy Layers For Sharing

Each Katchimera should have three useful copy layers.

### Layer 1: Name

This must be lovable and repeatable.

Good:

- `Lattelet`
- `Crumbun`
- `Neonpoko`
- `Mossprout`

### Layer 2: Formation reason

This explains what made it.

Examples:

- `Born from a warm cafe stop and a little wandering.`
- `Shaped by repeated park paths and green quiet.`
- `Formed from night routes and city glow.`

### Layer 3: Life reflection

This is the flattering emotional read.

Examples:

- `Warmth, routine, and a little curiosity shaped this one.`
- `Your familiar places are starting to leave a signature.`
- `Recovery became the part of the day that stayed visible.`

## 10. Share-Card Structure

If a Katchimera is meant to spread, the share card should communicate fast.

Recommended structure:

1. creature portrait
2. creature name
3. short reflection line
4. one reason tag or encounter cue

### Example

- `Lattelet`
- `Warmth, routine, and a little wandering shaped this one.`
- `Cafe regular`

That is better than surfacing raw tags or metrics.

## 11. What To Avoid

Avoid creatures that are:

- too abstract
- too stat-driven
- too symmetrical and generic
- too overloaded with motifs
- too random to explain
- too accurate in a cold or unflattering way

Avoid copy that sounds like:

- quantified-self dashboards
- therapy labels
- horoscope identity claims
- game loot flavor text

## 12. Practical System Recommendation

When generating a Katchimera, use this order:

1. detect the clearest encounter
2. detect the clearest emotional tone
3. detect the most flattering life-pattern meaning
4. build two to three visible motifs from those three layers
5. write one short reveal line that makes the user feel seen

If the creature cannot be summarized in one sentence, simplify it.

## 13. Decision Test

Before approving a Katchimera concept, ask:

1. Would the user know why they got it?
2. Would a friend understand it immediately?
3. Does it feel more like a self-portrait than a random drop?
4. Is the reflection flattering without feeling fake?
5. Would someone screenshot or post it without needing to explain the whole app?

If not, the creature is not shareable enough yet.

## 14. Final Direction

The most viral Katchimeras will not be the rarest ones.

They will be the ones that feel:

- cutest
- clearest
- most personally accurate
- easiest to explain

The winning formula is:

- `recognizable encounter`
- `clean mascot art`
- `specific life reflection`
- `short flattering copy`

That is what turns a generated creature into something people actually want to share.
